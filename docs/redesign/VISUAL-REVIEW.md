# Inferenco visual review and work order

**23 November 2026 · `feat/redesign-retheme` · local preview**

Build the Sound Flipper homepage card first. It is the smallest surface on which to judge the exposed metal rail, the screen angle, the blue ribbon and the amount of readable app detail. Use that proof to set the lighting and crop for the matching Resize & Compress card. Keep Blue Hour as the homepage reference, Collection as the catalogue reference and Signal as the product-page reference.

The illustrated review includes all four original mocks, matched browser captures, two exploratory handset compositions and a catalogue layout experiment. The illustrated PDF is a local review artifact at `output/pdf/inferenco-visual-review.pdf`.

## What the three independent reviewers changed

- **Editorial composition.** The catalogue can move substantially closer to Collection with the current assets. At 1440 × 900 a browser-only experiment brought the art row from y407 to y224 and the bottom of all three link targets to y885 or higher. Put this change into production alongside the art work.
- **Product imagery.** The strongest lean is in the two homepage teaser cards. The woodland phone has a gentle lean. Signal’s converter is almost face-on, and the catalogue shows frontal screens. Bake the handset lighting once for each pose and keep the source screen independently replaceable.
- **Responsive design.** A large desktop composition needs a distinct narrow crop. At 390 px the Sound handset is only 144 px wide. At 768 px each split home card has just 176 px for its copy. Devote the narrow artwork to the board, waveform or flower and put store controls in a quiet area.

The proposed production route combines the editorial layout with a studio-lit, offline-rendered chassis and replaceable screen artwork. The two handset studies in the PDF use CSS perspective and a separate illustrated rail. They demonstrate composition and scale. Photorealistic material, optically sharp supersampled output and final mobile crops are separate production work. A lower-cost delivery could ship prerendered versions of that layered technique if the rail, glass, screen and contact shadow hold up at actual size.

The mobile sources use their own framing and optimized AVIF with WebP fallback. Use the renderer's `--pose`, `--screen` and `--out` arguments to update one pose, with `--avif` for narrow exports.
